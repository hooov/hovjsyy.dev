Drops Tracker privacy policy

Effective November 24, 2026

Short version: Drops Tracker has no servers and collects nothing for its developer. It reads your Twitch session in your own browser so it can ask Twitch about your Drops progress. That data goes only to Twitch, and everything the extension keeps stays on your device.

This policy covers the Drops Tracker browser extension (the "extension"). It is made by an independent developer and is not affiliated with Twitch. The full source code is public at github.com/hooov/drops-tracker, so everything below can be checked.

What the extension accesses, and why

Your Twitch sign-in token

Twitch keeps your login in a browser cookie named auth-token. Once a minute, and once more when a channel page opens, the extension reads it and sends it to Twitch's own server (gql.twitch.tv), exactly as twitch.tv does, to request your Drops inventory and progress. The token is read fresh for each request. It is never saved, logged, shown, or sent anywhere other than Twitch. A short one-way fingerprint of it, which the token cannot be worked out from, is held in memory until the browser closes, only to notice another account signing in.

Your open Twitch tabs

The extension looks only at tabs on twitch.tv. From their addresses it learns which channels you have open. Inside those tabs it reads the state of the video player: whether it is playing, paused, muted, hidden or showing an ad, how far the video has advanced, and its resolution. This is how it works out why minutes have stopped. It does not read tabs on any other website.

Data Twitch returns

Twitch's answers include your Drops campaigns, minutes watched, unclaimed rewards, the channel Twitch is crediting, channel names, and campaign images. The images load from the addresses Twitch supplies, which are Twitch's own servers.

Twitch's own page requests

Twitch occasionally changes the identifiers its page uses to request data. To keep working, the extension reads the requests that twitch.tv pages send to gql.twitch.tv and keeps only those identifiers for the requests it uses. It also keeps a list of which requests Twitch's page makes, so a change on Twitch's side can be found: each request's name and identifier, the names of its settings and their types (yes/no settings as they were sent, never any other value), the kind of page it came from (a channel page, the Drops page, the home page, never which channel), when it was last seen, and how often. That list holds at most 150 requests, the oldest removed first. It does not read headers, so it never sees your token this way, and it keeps nothing else from those requests.

The Drops campaigns page

When you open the All Drops tab, press Load in the Badges tab, or visit twitch.tv/drops/campaigns yourself, the extension reads the campaign list from Twitch's response on that page. For the All Drops tab and for Load, it opens that page in a background tab when it needs a newer list, and closes it once the list has loaded. That page has no video.

Reward names and finished rewards on channel pages

Some reward campaigns give a different reward each day, and Twitch names each day's reward only in an answer its own channel page receives. On twitch.tv pages other than the Drops inventory page, the extension reads that one answer as Twitch's page receives it and keeps each day's reward name and image address, so the panel can show the same reward Twitch shows. The same answer says which reward drops you have finished; for some rewards, no other answer does. It keeps the identifiers of those reward drops, when their campaigns end and when it read them, so a finished campaign is not shown as one still to earn. It keeps no other progress from that answer, does not send it anywhere, and leaves every other request on the page untouched.

What it stores

Everything is kept in your browser's local extension storage, on your device only:

  • The latest tracking state: channels, drops, progress, and the current verdict.
  • A log of watched minutes, capped at one day (1,440 entries) with the oldest removed first. Each entry records the channel, whether Twitch credited the minute, and the player details listed above.
  • A record of the last 10 times Twitch credited a drop on a channel its own lists did not name for that drop: the channel, the drop and its campaign, and when. It is kept so a change on Twitch's side can be found. It has no time limit; the oldest is removed first.
  • Your settings.
  • The request identifiers learned from Twitch, the list of Twitch's page requests described above, the cached All Drops list, and the daily reward names and images described above, for at most 20 campaigns, each removed when its campaign ends.
  • Which reward drops Twitch says you have finished, described above: at most 100, each removed when its campaign ends.
  • The badge campaigns seen on channels you opened, so the Badges tab can list the badges you have not earned: their names, dates, images, details links, categories, badge names, watch minutes and repeat settings. At most 100, each removed when its campaign ends, and never which channel.

None of this leaves your device unless you choose to copy it. Removing the extension deletes all of it.

Who it is shared with

Only Twitch, and only to show you your Drops progress. Twitch's handling of that data is covered by the Twitch Privacy Notice.

The extension has no analytics, no tracking, no ads, and no server of its own. The developer receives no data from it. No data is sold, and none is shared with any other party.

Things you do yourself

  • Copy debug info and Copy log, both shown only with Developer mode on in the Settings tab, copy to your clipboard only when you click them. They are built from a fixed list of fields and never include your token, but they do include which channels you watched, minute by minute, for up to a day, the channels in the record of credited drops above, and your progress. If you paste them into a bug report, they become public on GitHub.
  • Notifications are shown by your browser on your device. The one for a drop ready to claim can be switched off with Claim reminders in the Settings tab. The one saying a drop with no claim step was probably earned is shown once per drop, the one saying a reward campaign is finished once per campaign, and neither can be switched off.
  • Auto-refresh of the Drops page and auto-reload of a stalled stream tab are both on by default, can be switched off in the Settings tab, and only reload Twitch tabs you already have open.

Chrome Web Store Limited Use

Drops Tracker's use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. User data is used only to provide the extension's single purpose: tracking your Twitch Drops, which means showing whether Twitch is actually crediting your Drops watch-minutes, warning you and helping when it stops, and listing the Drops you can earn. It is not used for advertising, not sold, and not read by any person.

Children

The extension is meant for Twitch users and does not knowingly collect anything about anyone, children included.

Changes
